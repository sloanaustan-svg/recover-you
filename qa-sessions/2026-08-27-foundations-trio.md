# Foundations Trio — Q&A Session (2026-08-27)

Personal reflections tied to three pages on Recover-You: **What Is Trauma**, **What Is C-PTSD**, and **The Disease of Addiction**. Answers are raw — intended as source material for later revision into the site itself.

## What Is Trauma

### Q1. The page draws a hard line between "trauma that happened to a self" (the crash — bounded, with a before/after) and "trauma that built one" (no before at all). Can you give an example of an adult moment where you caught yourself reacting from that second kind of wiring — something disproportionate in the moment that made total sense once you understood the blueprint?

I can't — and I think that really illuminates the problem. My only starting point was the most tragic question — "what the fuck is wrong with me?". Feeling not just that the world doesn't make any sense, but that I don't make sense within myself. It's hard to pinpoint the problem when the 'problem' is merely a sense that you (I) am wrong. Everything elicited a disproportionate response, because that was all that I was shown. Just survive.

### Q2. You write "the abuse I could name. Being abandoned laid a set of tracks I didn't choose." What did that abandonment actually look like day to day, and how long did it take you to realize it was doing more damage than the abuse itself?

I believe, in retrospect, that this showed up as me abandoning myself pathologically. Because I internalized the belief that I wasn't worth anything and it occurred before language and cognition, I didn't necessarily think "I'm worthless" to myself. I just acted like I was. That's a harder thread to pull — because it's not always obvious. I was shown that I had no value, so I acted in accordance with it — the cost has no calculable sum. What is one's own self worth to one's self?

### Q3. The page uses the frog-in-slowly-heating-water image — dysfunction as baseline, safety feeling "wrong." Was there a specific moment or person that first showed you the contrast — that made a healthy environment register as strange rather than safe?

Going to my friends' houses. Seeing all of their toys, new (and clean) clothes, toys and organization. Doting parents, offering hugs and asking if I needed anything (to which I would always say 'no', I think because I wanted to 'show' that I needed nothing).

## What Is C-PTSD

### Q4. Of the six ICD-11 domains (re-experiencing, avoidance, sense of current threat, affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, disturbed relationships) — which one hit hardest for you, and what did it actually look like in ordinary daily life?

Negative self concept, followed closely by disturbed relationships. Imagine existing in the depth of heavy addiction — hiding it from everyone, all the while detesting the person you see in the mirror. Constantly feeling the slow pull of the loss of control, the hopelessness in knowing you can't climb out, and pretending to everyone around you that you have it all together — somehow managing to keep it all between the lines and effectively fooling everyone but to your own peril. To top it off, balancing personal intimate relationships that are caught in this chaotic duality, all the while ready to vanish at the drop of a hat to prevent being hurt, even though I'm the one causing all the pain.

### Q5. You mention finding Tim Fletcher's talk and finally feeling "oriented rather than overwhelmed." What was going on in your life right before you found that video, and what shifted in the weeks after?

I first watched those when I got out of treatment in Claresholm. Tim presented himself as an addictions counsellor, and in one of his talks he alluded to the idea that virtually everyone he treated in (severe) addiction had complex trauma. His breakdowns of concepts — from shame to self-destruction and relationships — were spoken in such a way that I felt as though I was finally being handed the manual to myself.

### Q6. The page notes people often get routed into ADHD/depression/anxiety diagnoses instead of C-PTSD. Did you carry one of those labels first? What did it get wrong, and how long before you found a better frame?

My stepmother was quick to get me on ADHD meds when I was around 10–12 years old. I was totally unmanageable. In retrospect it makes perfect sense — I had just gone through an absolutely tragic childhood and she didn't have the means to get through to me. Both her children were well adjusted. Out of a joint family of six (2 parents, 4 kids) I was the nightmare. It was tagged as ADHD, I was medicated — it 'worked' temporarily, but it was a band-aid. I did a bit better in school, but not everywhere else. The reality — what was being observed was a child that felt wholly inadequate and that he didn't belong.

## Disease of Addiction

### Q7. The page ends by asking the reader "where did yours begin?" without answering it for yourself — what is your own Genesis Point?

That is the question isn't it. For me, my Genesis Point was at the apex of the abuse and abandonment. The point of original injury. Not when I picked up the first drugs years later.

### Q8. You describe the fire being out but the alarm still going — sobriety revealing the older pain underneath. Was there a specific stretch of your own sobriety where that was most acute? What did it look like?

Anxiety and unprovoked panic attacks. When I began to realize that I struggled doing normal everyday things. What do you mean people don't mentally prepare the route they will take in traffic and which lanes they will travel in before they get in the car? What do you mean people don't only go to grocery stores immediately when they open or close to avoid the 'busyness' of the store? What do you mean people don't walk away from the registry because it's busy even though they need to renew their license? What do you mean people don't go to the gym to build muscle just so that people will leave them alone? What do you mean employees will avoid required interactions for fear of meeting new people (and hide in a bathroom stall at work to do so)?
