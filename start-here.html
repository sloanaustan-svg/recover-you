How to navigate Recover-You

Start Here

Ten phases, in a deliberate order. This is the map I needed twenty-five years ago.

// Stop Guessing. Follow the Logic.

Recover-You is a free, survivor-built psychoeducation resource for people navigating trauma and addiction recovery. It is organized as a deliberate roadmap, not a collection of articles.

In crisis or in need of treatment right now?

Stabilization comes first. Skip the roadmap and go directly to Crisis Support. You can also use the Treatment Centre Directory to begin getting your name on waitlists. You cannot renovate a house that is still on fire.

Recovery feels impossible when you are trying to solve symptoms without understanding the system that created them. You are told to change your behaviour, build resilience, and try harder without ever being shown what happened, what adapted, or why it still shows up today.

This site is written primarily for people carrying the compounded weight of trauma and addiction, but it is not exclusive to that experience. Trauma can take hold in the body and shape behaviour whether addiction is present or not. Every page works toward one goal: helping you name what happened and understand how it shaped the patterns you are now trying to change. If you are in Alberta and looking for care, the Alberta system navigation guide is here too.

This is the map I desperately needed 25 years ago. I had no idea where to turn or even what questions to ask. I cannot promise smooth sailing, but I can promise you will be better prepared for what lies ahead than I ever was.

// If this is not your first attempt

I know what it feels like to believe I have failed before I have even started. That feeling intensified every time I restarted recovery. Each attempt seemed to begin with more evidence against me, courtesy of the last spectacular relapse.

I see relapse differently now. A relapse is evidence that something in the plan failed. It is not proof that you are a failure. Ask who, what, when, where, and why. Use the answers to change the path forward.

Left unexamined, relapse becomes a painful millstone engraved with our supposed inadequacy. Examined honestly, it becomes data. It can strengthen recovery instead of dismantling it.

// Start Here

A Guided Order, for a Reason

Recover-You moves through three deliberate layers. Knowing that upfront changes how you experience it.

01

Education, grounded in lived experience

The science is woven together with my own relationship to it, because concepts land differently when you can feel them. The goal is recognition. You begin to see yourself in the patterns, release some shame, and understand the why behind what you have been carrying.

02

A critical look at the systems meant to help

This is not cynicism. It is validation. If treatment never mentioned trauma, or “trauma-informed care” meant very little in practice, your frustration is real and widely shared. You are not crazy. You are not broken. These are systemic failures, not personal ones.

03

The path forward

Evidence-based therapies, practical tools, and a resource centre that moves from detox to trauma-specific care. Programs are explained clearly and linked directly to application pages where available.

You do not have to read it on a screen. Many of the longer pages can be downloaded as free, printable PDFs, formatted so you can read offline, mark them up, or pass them to someone who needs them. Look for the PDF tag in the roadmap below.

When circumstances allow, follow the sequence. Each section builds on the last. You can arrive completely lost and leave with a clearer understanding of what is happening, what may help, and where to take the next practical step.

Trauma is a fact of life.
It does not have to be a life sentence.

Peter Levine

Understand trauma beyond labels: what it is, how it shapes the nervous system, and why it drives so many adult patterns.

  • Attachment StylesPDF How early relationships shape expectations around safety, closeness, trust, and self-worth.
  • Toxic StressPDF How prolonged stress without a buffering adult reshapes the brain and body, creating a biological foundation for later struggles.

  • The Dunedin Study Landmark research tracking how early temperament and self-control relate to adult outcomes across decades.

The biological mechanisms that make trauma and addiction feel so persistent, and what can change them.

Why this matters: Understanding the “why” behind your symptoms can reduce shame and give you a target. This section explains how trauma and addiction reshape the brain and nervous system, and why sobriety alone is often not enough.

  • Brain on FirePDF How trauma vulnerability and addiction create a self-reinforcing loop in the brain.
  • When Sobriety Feels WorsePDF Why removing the substance can expose a dysregulated nervous system and make early recovery feel unbearable.
  • NeuroplasticityPDF How the brain wires for survival and addiction, and how it can rewire for recovery.
  • Critical WindowsPDF Why early childhood is a powerful period for brain development and why missed developmental needs can be difficult to address later.
  • EpigeneticsPDF How environment can influence gene expression without changing DNA, shaping vulnerability and resilience.
  • Why Trauma Doesn't ExpirePDF What can happen biologically and neurologically when trauma goes unaddressed across a lifetime, and why intervention still matters.

  • MetabolismPDF Why people respond differently to substances, medications, and stress based on individual biology.
  • The Hijacked EnginePDF How trauma can make the brain’s reward system more vulnerable to addiction and cue-driven behaviour.

Treating Trauma and Addiction TogetherThe evidence for integrated care

A focused look at co-occurring PTSD and substance use disorder, and why integrated care is becoming harder to dismiss.

Why this comes last in this section: After looking at trauma, the nervous system, toxic stress, and the biology of addiction, this page shifts from mechanism to treatment evidence. Project Harmony does not prove that trauma causes every addiction, and it does not argue for rushing people into trauma work. What it does show is harder to ignore: when PTSD and substance use disorder overlap, keeping them in separate treatment silos by default is no longer well supported by the evidence.

  • Treating Trauma and Addiction TogetherPDF What Project Harmony clarifies about PTSD, substance use, integrated care, clinical caution, and the limits of the old “addiction first, trauma later” model.

Therapeutic ModelsWhat actually helps

Structured approaches that target thinking, emotion regulation, trauma patterns, and identity repair.

Why this matters: Insight alone rarely changes behaviour. Progress usually requires structured methods that retrain thinking, regulate the nervous system, and challenge long-held survival patterns. This section explains what different therapies do and when they may be useful.

  • CBTPDF Understanding the link between thoughts, emotions, and behaviour, and learning to interrupt distorted thinking patterns.
  • DBTPDF Distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and learning to sit with urges without escaping into old coping strategies.
  • Life Traps (Schema Therapy)PDF Core beliefs formed in childhood that can silently run adult behaviour, and how to challenge them.
  • Healing Trauma (Post-Traumatic Growth)PDF Healing as integration rather than erasure, and what growth can mean over time.
  • Therapy TypesPDF A guide to common trauma-focused and behavioural therapies, what they target, and how they differ.
  • The Role of TherapyPDF What therapy is for, what it can and cannot do, and why commitment matters alongside modality.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: March 2026

If you reach out through the contact form, I'll collect your name, email, and message — nothing more. That information is used only to get back to you. It's not shared, sold, or used for anything else.

This site uses two analytics tools to help me understand how people find and use it. Google Analytics is only active if you choose to allow analytics cookies. Netlify Web Analytics runs quietly in the background using general server data — no cookies, no individual tracking.

A few third-party services help keep the site running — things like hosting and the contact form. Some of these are based outside Canada, so your information may be processed there. I've chosen providers I trust, but I want you to know.

Please don't share anything urgent or crisis-related through the contact form — it's not monitored in real time. If you're in immediate distress, please reach out to a crisis line or emergency services.

Questions about this policy, or want to see or correct something I have about you? You can reach me through the contact page — I'm happy to help.

Optional analytics: I use optional analytics to understand how people use the site, where pages lose people, and what needs improvement. You can decline and still use the site normally. If you decline, I may ask again later in case your preferences change.
